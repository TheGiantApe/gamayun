> DPI AND MARGINS, THE PART MOST CHARTS SKIP

A dimension alone doesn't tell you what resolution to design at. For print, 300 DPI (dots per inch) is the standard target for anything viewed up close (documents, photos); 150 DPI is usually fine for large-format posters viewed from a distance, since the eye can't resolve the extra detail anyway. At 300 DPI, an A4 page (8.3 × 11.7 in) needs a canvas of roughly 2481 × 3510 pixels; US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) needs roughly 2550 × 3300.

Every A-series size is exactly half the area of the size above it, cut along the long edge (A4 folded in half makes two A5 sheets) - the same aspect ratio (1:√2) is preserved at every step, which is why A-series paper stacks and scales so cleanly and North American sizes don't (Letter and Legal share a width but not the same ratio, and neither shares a ratio with Tabloid).

A safe default print margin is 0.5 in (12.7 mm) on all sides for home/office printers - most consumer printers can't reliably print closer to the edge than that without risk of clipping, regardless of what the page size itself allows.
